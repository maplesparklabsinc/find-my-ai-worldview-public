# Overview

## The idea

Most AI quizzes and debates sort people into "pro" and "anti", or "doomer" and "accelerationist". Published research says the public does not divide that way: most people expect both benefits and harms, many are undecided, and views on pace, rules, human decision-making and fairness do not line up neatly with optimism. Find My AI Worldview helps people put their own mix of views into words, see the trade-off that matters most to them, and find real common ground with people who land elsewhere.

Goals, in order: self-understanding; better conversations across different views; honest public education about what people actually think; opt-in research that can test whether these perspectives are real.

## Who it is for

The general English-speaking public in Canada, the US, the UK and Australia, including people who rarely use AI. Not AI insiders.

## What the respondent gets

A profile on six constructs (expected benefit, expected harm, pace, who sets the rules, who decides, who benefits); the closest of seven working perspectives with the next two nearest, or an honest Undecided, Mixed or Indifferent outcome; the defining tension in their own answers; common ground and differences; how their views compare with published public opinion in their country; a way to compare with friends; a way to challenge the result.

## Decisions so far

See `decisions.md`. Headlines: working name Find My AI Worldview on a Maple Spark Labs subdomain; simple, friendly flow with results and sharing and no sign-up; every completion logged anonymously; optional email for future research contact; dimensional profile with a "closest perspective" overlay; general English-speaking public; neutral English; standard form only in v1; Next.js, Supabase (Canada), Vercel.
