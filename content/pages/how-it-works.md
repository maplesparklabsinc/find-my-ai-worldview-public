### What this is

Find My AI Worldview helps you figure out what you actually think about AI, and talk about it with other people. You answer some quick questions, and we show you where you land on six things people disagree about, the perspective your answers are closest to, and what stands out about your views.

It's not a test, there are no right answers, and no view is better than another. It's not a political quiz either: views on AI don't line up neatly with left and right.

### The six things we ask about

1. How much good will AI do?
2. How much harm will AI do? (We ask these two separately because lots of people expect plenty of both.)
3. How fast should AI move?
4. Who should set the rules: companies and markets, or governments and independent watchdogs?
5. Who should make decisions about people: AI, where it does better, or always a human?
6. Who should share in the gains?

Each one gets five statements. Some are written from one side and some from the other, so just agreeing with everything doesn't push you anywhere.

### How your result is worked out

For each of the six, we average your answers and place you somewhere between the two sides. Then we look at seven perspectives, each with a typical position on those six things, and show you the one you're closest to, plus the next two.

If you're about equally close to two, we tell you that. If you're not close to any of them, we tell you that too. And if you said your views are still forming, or you skipped a lot, we say so instead of forcing a label on you.

Just before their result, most people are asked where they think their answers landed. It's optional, and your guess never changes your result. We look at the guesses only in total, to learn how people's expectations compare with what their answers show.

Your answers about yourself (where you live, how much you use AI and so on) never change your result. We only use where you live to show you how people in your country answered similar questions in published polls.

One question near the end asks about rules for low-risk uses of AI; it helps us improve the perspectives and doesn't change your result.

The full rules are public: [the scoring rules]({scoring_rules_url}).

### About the seven perspectives

The perspectives are Accelerators, Stewards, Precautionists, Skeptics, Human-Control Defenders, Shared-Gains Advocates and Pragmatists. They're our best description of the main positions people take, based on published research and public debate. Researchers who sort the public by attitude usually find three to five groups, based on how much good and harm people expect; ours add who should set the rules, who should decide and who should benefit. They're not scientific categories, and they will change as we learn from how people actually answer. Each one comes with the strongest objection to it, because that's part of describing a view fairly.

Right now your result is measured against a fixed scale. Once enough people have taken it, we'll compare each answer with how everyone else answered, and some results will shift. If yours would change, the page will say so and link to what changed.

### Where the numbers come from

When we show how people in your country answered, the source, year and who was asked are right there. Those figures come from published polls, not from this quiz, and the questions they asked weren't always worded the same way as ours. We say "a similar question" when that's the case.

### How this was made

This is a project by Maple Spark Labs in Toronto, led by Walter Varma-Chang. It was inspired by FindMyPolitics, a political quiz whose creator is open about how its categories have changed over time.

We used AI a lot, and here's how:

- **ChatGPT** (OpenAI) wrote the first draft of the research plan, including an early list of ten "tribes" and some question ideas.
- **Claude** (Anthropic) checked that draft against the original sources, found research it had missed, cut the ten tribes down to six perspectives, then added a seventh, Skeptics, after reviewing how researchers and the press describe the debate, wrote the current questions, the scoring rules and most of the words on this site, and is helping build it.
- **Walter** decided what to keep, change or drop, and is responsible for all of it.

AI-written text isn't evidence. The sources behind this project were checked against the originals and are listed with their dates and who they surveyed. [Sources]({sources_url})
